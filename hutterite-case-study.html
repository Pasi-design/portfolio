Overview

Two products, one design system — buyer storefront and vendor dashboard

Hutterite Marketplace is a Canadian multi-vendor marketplace that connects buyers directly with Hutterite colonies and local businesses. It covers farm-fresh produce, meat and eggs, handcrafted goods, farm and industrial equipment, building materials, and skilled trade services such as welding, roofing and custom builds.

I designed the UI/UX for two connected products: the buyer-facing e-commerce storefront, where customers browse, compare, order and request quotes — and the vendor dashboard, where colonies and businesses manage products, orders and enquiries with full CRUD.

Scope of work

User Research Information Architecture Wireframing UI Design Design System Prototyping Responsive Web Vendor Dashboard (CRUD)

Problem

Real friction on both sides of the marketplace

Hutterite colonies produce high-quality goods and skilled services, but selling them beyond local pickup and word of mouth is hard. The marketplace had to solve distinct problems on both sides.

Buyer challenges
01

A huge, mixed catalogue — about 317 products across 14 departments, from pumpkins to marine dock equipment, plus services and wholesale. Without strong structure, shoppers feel lost.

02

Trust — buyers needed to know who they were buying from and that the seller was a real, verified colony or business.

03

Location-dependent perishables — fresh food is only delivered in select cities (Edmonton, Calgary, Red Deer and others), so showing items a buyer can't receive causes frustration.

04

Not everything is "add to cart" — services and custom work need quotes, and some buyers need items nobody lists yet.

Vendor challenges
05

Low digital confidence — many colony users aren't professional e-commerce operators, so a complex admin panel would mean abandoned listings and missed orders.

06

Multiple departments per colony — a single colony may run carpentry, market garden, poultry and metalwork shops, each needing its own enquiries and stock.

07

Operational load — vendors need to create listings, track orders, update stock and handle quotes without training.

How might we make a complex multi-vendor marketplace feel simple, trustworthy and local for buyers — while giving vendors a dashboard they can use confidently from day one?

Process

Six phases from discovery to high-fidelity

01 — Research & Discovery

Audit both user groups

Audited the business model and both user groups — buyers (households, farms, contractors, wholesale) and sellers (colonies, local businesses). Benchmarked marketplaces and B2B grocery platforms for navigation, filtering and vendor tooling. Mapped the full product range, delivery rules and order flows to find where the experience could break.

02 — Personas & User Flows

Map every buying intent

Created personas for the city household buyer, the farmer sourcing equipment, the wholesale buyer and the colony manager. Mapped key flows: browse → set location → add to cart → checkout; post a request → receive offers → accept; vendor adds a product → receives an order → fulfils it.

03 — Information Architecture

Three clear entry points

Organised the catalogue into shop by department, shop by colony or business, and services. Designed a mega-menu with category counts and thumbnails. Created a delivery-location selector so availability is set once and applied everywhere across the experience.

04 — Wireframes → UI Design

Low-fi to high-fidelity

Started with low-fidelity wireframes of the home page, product listing, product detail, cart and dashboard screens. Built a design system with colours, typography, product cards, status badges and form patterns so the storefront and dashboard feel like one product across all responsive breakpoints.

05 — Dashboard Design (CRUD)

Designed for non-technical vendors

Create: a simple product form with variants, price ranges, images, stock and category. Read: an order list with status filters and search. Update: quick edits without leaving the list. Delete/archive: confirmation dialogs and safe alternatives to permanent deletion. Used plain language, clear empty states and inline validation throughout.

06 — Prototype & Iterate

Test and refine key flows

Built clickable prototypes of the key buyer and vendor flows in Figma, tested them, and refined the designs based on the feedback received.

Solution

Two experiences, built as one product

01

Location-first shopping

A "Choose location" control sets delivery once and shows fresh-delivery cities, so buyers only ever see what they can actually receive. Perishable frustration — gone from the first interaction.

02

Department-led navigation

Image-based department tiles with item counts and a searchable filter make a 300+ item catalogue easy to scan. The mega-menu shows category shape before a user clicks — reducing pogo-sticking between the home page and product lists.

03

Trust built into every card

Every product card shows the colony name. The home page carries the statement "Every seller is a real colony or local business, verified by us." Dedicated colony pages and a map directory make the people behind each product visible — converting first-time visitors into confident buyers.

04

Beyond add-to-cart

Quote buttons for trades and services, a Post a Request feature where colonies send offers, live auctions, and a wholesale/B2B entry point serve every buying intent — not just the straightforward purchase.

05

Vendor dashboard designed for day one

A focused dashboard where colonies create, view, edit and delete products and manage orders from one place. Department-aware structure routes enquiries to the right shop. Simple, forgiving forms and clear status labels are designed for non-technical users.

Design approach

A warm, community-driven look with strong photography and a clean, scannable layout — so a large catalogue feels approachable rather than overwhelming. One design system covering the storefront, vendor dashboard and service flows ensures both experiences feel like one product, not two bolted together.

A warm, community-driven look with strong photography and a clean, scannable layout.
Clear product cards — price or price range, sale %, stock state and one-tap "Add to cart" or "Choose options" — reduce hesitation at the point of decision.
Reassurance at key moments: secure checkout, direct-from-colony messaging and pickup/delivery notes sit exactly where buyers need them.

Result

A live marketplace used by colonies across Canada

What the design delivered

A live, working marketplace covering two full user experiences — buyer storefront and vendor dashboard — built as one cohesive product.
One design system covering the storefront, vendor dashboard and service flows, so every screen speaks the same visual language.
A scalable information architecture that makes 317+ products across 14 departments easy to browse, filter and find.
A location-first delivery system that eliminates the frustration of showing buyers products they can't receive.
A vendor CRUD dashboard designed so non-technical colony users can create, edit and manage listings and orders with confidence.

Key learnings

With a large catalogue, strong information architecture matters more than visual polish.
Designing for less technical vendors means fewer fields, plain language and forgiving flows.
Trust signals such as real seller identity and clear delivery rules are what turn first-time visitors into buyers.
