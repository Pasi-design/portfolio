01 / Overview

Two sides of a marketplace. One connected product.

Hutterite Marketplace is a Canadian multi-vendor marketplace connecting buyers directly with Hutterite colonies and local businesses. Its catalogue spans farm-fresh produce, meat and eggs, handcrafted goods, farm and industrial equipment, building materials and skilled trade services.

I designed the UI/UX for two connected experiences: a buyer-facing storefront to browse, compare, order and request quotes, and a vendor dashboard for colonies and businesses to manage products, orders and enquiries.

✳

The design opportunity
Make a large, varied catalogue feel easy to navigate for buyers — while keeping everyday listing and order-management tasks approachable for vendors.

02 / The challenge

Make complexity feel manageable.

The platform had to serve buyers with different shopping needs and vendors with different levels of digital confidence. The design needed to solve for both sides without making the experience feel fragmented.

BUYER / 01

A broad catalogue

Hundreds of products across 14 departments need clear structure, useful filtering and intuitive discovery.

BUYER / 02

Trust and availability

Buyers need to understand who is selling and whether location-dependent fresh products can reach them.

BUYER / 03

Different purchase intents

Services, custom work and wholesale enquiries do not always fit a standard add-to-cart journey.

VENDOR / 01

Low digital confidence

Complex admin tools can discourage vendors from adding listings or managing orders consistently.

VENDOR / 02

Multiple departments

A single colony may operate several shops, each with different products, stock and enquiries.

VENDOR / 03

Daily operations

Vendors need straightforward ways to create listings, update stock, track orders and respond to quotes.

?

Core design question
How might we make a complex multi-vendor marketplace feel simple, trustworthy and local for buyers — while giving vendors a dashboard they can use confidently?

03 / Design process

From discovery to a connected platform.

The work moved through six phases, balancing the needs of marketplace shoppers with the practical tasks vendors complete every day.

PHASE 01

Research & discovery

Audited the business model and user groups, benchmarked marketplace patterns, and mapped catalogue, delivery and order requirements.

PHASE 02

Personas & flows

Mapped household, farm, wholesale and colony-manager needs alongside browse, checkout, request and fulfilment journeys.

PHASE 03

Information architecture

Structured entry points around departments, colonies and services, with location selection and searchable navigation.

PHASE 04

Wireframes to UI

Designed key storefront and dashboard screens, then established reusable components and responsive patterns.

PHASE 05

Vendor dashboard

Designed create, view, edit and archive workflows for products, alongside order lists, status filters and clear forms.

PHASE 06

Prototype & iterate

Built clickable Figma prototypes for buyer and vendor journeys and refined the experience using received feedback.

04 / The experience

Designed around real marketplace behaviour.

The solution connects discovery, confidence and operations — helping buyers find the right offer while giving vendors practical tools to manage their presence.

01

Location-first shopping

A “Choose location” control sets delivery once and applies availability across the experience, helping buyers focus on products they can actually receive.

02

Department-led navigation

Image-based department tiles, item counts, search and filters make a 300+ item catalogue easier to scan and explore.

03

Trust built into the storefront

Colony names on product cards, verified-seller messaging, dedicated colony pages and a map directory help make the people behind each product visible.

04

More than add-to-cart

Quote requests, post-a-request workflows, auctions and wholesale entry points support purchase intents beyond standard checkout.

05

A vendor dashboard for day-to-day work

Focused create, view, edit and delete/archive workflows help vendors manage products and orders with plain language, clear status labels and forgiving forms.

05 / Design approach

A warm, consistent system for both sides.

The visual direction uses a community-driven feel, strong product photography and a clean, scannable layout so a large catalogue feels approachable. A shared design system connects the storefront, vendor dashboard and service flows instead of making them feel like separate products.

One marketplace.
Consistent patterns.

Shared colour, typography, product cards, status badges, forms and responsive rules help keep buyer and vendor experiences aligned.

06 / Outcome & reflection

A live marketplace with room to grow.

The platform brings together buyer shopping and vendor operations, supported by a structured catalogue, location-aware availability and multiple ways to engage with sellers.

What the design delivered

A live marketplace covering two connected user experiences: the buyer storefront and vendor dashboard.
A shared design system across storefront, vendor dashboard and service flows.
A scalable information architecture for products across 14 departments.
A location-first delivery approach for products with geographic availability limits.
Vendor CRUD workflows designed to make listing and order management more approachable.

Key learnings

For a large catalogue, information architecture is as important as the visual interface.
Designing for less technical vendors calls for fewer fields, plain language and forgiving workflows.
Trust signals — real seller identity and clear delivery rules — need to appear at the moments buyers make decisions.
✳

What I took away
A marketplace is not just a storefront. It is a system of relationships, operations and decisions. The strongest experience makes those connections feel clear for everyone involved.
