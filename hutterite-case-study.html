Overview

A commerce platform built around community identity

Hutterite is an e-commerce platform serving a distinctive community brand. The project required designing an experience that balanced the community's unique cultural identity with the usability standards modern shoppers expect — making it easy to browse, discover and purchase products while feeling native to the brand's values.

The scope covered the full UX and UI lifecycle: from competitor research and user interviews through to a production-ready design system and high-fidelity interface.

01

Community customers who value authentic, brand-aligned shopping experiences above speed.

02

First-time visitors discovering the brand who need fast, clear product storytelling.

03

Repeat buyers who expect a reliable, low-friction checkout every time.

Problem

Generic e-commerce templates were eroding the brand

The existing platform felt indistinguishable from any off-the-shelf store. It lacked visual personality, had an inconsistent information hierarchy across pages, and the checkout flow caused measurable drop-off at the payment step. Users reported confusion about product availability, shipping policies and order status.

01

Visual identity mismatch — the interface looked generic and failed to reflect the community brand.

02

Product pages lacked hierarchy — customers struggled to find key details before deciding to buy.

03

Checkout drop-off — payment and shipping steps were unclear, causing abandoned carts.

Research findings

Brand trust is established through visual consistency — users expected the store to look and feel as distinctive as the products.
Product storytelling matters — customers want to know the origin and values behind what they're buying.
Checkout clarity directly impacts conversion — transparency on price, shipping and delivery timeline reduces abandonment.
Mobile-first experience is non-negotiable — over 65% of traffic came from mobile devices.

Process

Discovery to high-fidelity in four structured phases

01 — Research

Competitor audit & user interviews

Reviewed six competitor e-commerce platforms and ran stakeholder and user interviews to map the shopping journey and surface key drop-off points.

02 — Define

Information architecture & user flows

Mapped the full site structure and key user flows: product discovery, product detail, cart and checkout — ensuring every path was clear and decision points were minimised.

03 — Design

Wireframes → design system → high-fidelity

Lo-fi wireframes validated the layout logic. A comprehensive design system was built first, then high-fidelity screens were produced page by page in Figma.

04 — Validate

Usability testing & iteration

Two rounds of moderated usability testing refined the product page layout and the three-step checkout flow before final sign-off.

Solution

Four design directions that shaped every page

01

Brand-led visual identity throughout

Every page uses a consistent colour system, typography scale and imagery style drawn directly from the brand guidelines — making the store feel native to the community.

02

Product pages built around the decision moment

Key information — product name, price, availability and shipping estimate — sits above the fold on every product page. Supporting content follows in a scannable hierarchy below.

03

Three-step checkout with full transparency

Cart review, address and payment, and order confirmation are three clearly separated steps. Running totals, shipping costs and delivery estimates are visible at every stage.

04

Mobile-first responsive system

Every component was designed mobile-first and tested across four breakpoints. Navigation, product grids and checkout adapt cleanly from 375px to 1440px.

Result

Outcomes & what the work delivered

0Reduction in checkout abandonment after the flow redesign
0Average usability score in testing across both rounds
0Client satisfaction — signed off on first full review

What the final design delivered

A brand-led visual identity that made the store feel native to the community and its values.
Clear product page hierarchy that reduced time-to-decision and improved conversion.
A transparent three-step checkout that reduced friction and cut abandonment at the payment step.
A scalable design system the development team could build from directly without design support.
A fully responsive interface tested across all major breakpoints and device types.
